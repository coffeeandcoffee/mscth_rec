Prior EEG research supports the idea that neural signals can be used to infer latent cognitive states and relate them to behavior, rather than treating EEG as a direct readout of overt actions alone \cite{bridwell_moving_2018, vo_deep_2024}. In that sense, the present study is positioned as a first step: it does not claim to measure long-term behavior change directly, but it does show that naturalistic content exposure is accompanied by distinguishable neural patterns during imminent stay-versus-skip decisions, which is consistent with the broader view that EEG can capture intermediate psychological processes that may later shape learning, preference formation, and behavior over time \cite{bridwell_moving_2018, vo_deep_2024}.
