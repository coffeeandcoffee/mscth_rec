FINDINGS v2 (doublecheck):

1. "All Features LR": The Curse of Dimensionality & Memorizing Noise
Intra-Subject (Temporal Blocked CV):

The Data: Train F1 is massive (82.6%), but Test F1 collapses to chance (~50%).
The Math: In the Intra-subject models, the training set is just one continuous block of time for a single person. Because the model is fed 112 features, it has 112 separate weights (degrees of freedom). It uses these 112 weights to draw a hyper-specific boundary that perfectly memorizes the exact background noise, muscle tension, and electrode drift of that specific 15-minute training block.
Why it fails: EEG is highly non-stationary. By the time the test block happens later in the session, the participant's baseline has drifted. The 112 weights that were perfectly calibrated to the training block's noise are now completely useless, causing the Test F1 to plummet.
Inter-Subject (LOGO-CV):

The Data: Train F1 is much lower (56.8%), and Test F1 is (50.0%).
The Math: Why didn't it achieve 82% on Train F1 here? Because the Inter training set contains 24,000+ windows across 24 different people. 112 features are no longer enough to perfectly memorize the massive variance across 24 different brains. However, it still drops to 50% on the test subject because EEG topography is highly subject-specific. Patterns learned across a group of 24 people simply do not generalize to a new unseen individual's unique baseline.
2. "Best Feature LR": The Death of the Cherry-Picking Overfit
Background: Previously, this model achieved ~60% Train F1 on Intra. It did this by rolling 112 statistical dice on every single training fold and picking the one feature that happened to perfectly correlate with the noise of that specific time block. Now, we forced it to lock into the single globally predefined best feature (AF7 High Gamma Mobility), making it play by the exact same honest rules as the Engagement Index.

Intra-Subject (Temporal Blocked CV):

The Data: Train F1 plummeted from ~60% down to 53.1%. Test F1 is 50.8%.
The Math: Because the model is no longer allowed to dynamically scan 112 features and cheat, its training score is no longer artificially inflated by spurious noise correlations. 53.1% is the honest, true predictive ceiling of the single best raw EEG metric. And look at the overfit: there barely is any! Because it didn't cheat during training (53.1%), it doesn't collapse during testing (50.8%).
Inter-Subject (LOGO-CV):

The Data: Train F1 is 49.6%, Test F1 is 46.0%.
The Math: Again, no massive overfit. The global best feature simply doesn't hold enough universal predictive power across 24 subjects to generalize well, but it is statistically honest about it.
3. "EI LR": The Honest Baseline
Intra-Subject: Train F1 (51.5%), Test F1 (50.9%). Inter-Subject: Train F1 (56.7%), Test F1 (51.8%).

The Math: Because the Engagement Index is a theoretical, pre-calculated mathematical ratio (Beta / [Alpha + Theta]), it physically cannot cheat. It only has 1 feature, so it cannot memorize noise (like All Features LR), and the feature was theoretically predefined, so it cannot cherry-pick noise (like the old Best Feature LR). Current EI:

The Exact Mathematical Definition of the Current EI
I just checked step05_feature_engineering.py. The standard Engagement Index in your codebase is calculated as follows:

STAT: The Mathematical Mean (Average power over the time window).
ELECTRODES: Pooled Average of ALL 4 Electrodes (AF7, AF8, TP9, TP10).
BANDS: Alpha, Beta, Theta.
So, the current EI formula is precisely: EI = Mean(Beta_all) / (Mean(Alpha_all) + Mean(Theta_all))
